1. Operational Framework & Silence Protocol

This playbook establishes a rigorous, evidence-anchored methodology for evaluating public, vendor, briefing, and media claims regarding the Raytheon Standard Missile-6 (SM-6 / RIM-174 Extended Range Active Missile).

Defense analysis frequently degenerates into "specification dumping"—reciting physical parameters, factory throughput goals, or marketing concepts without assessing kill-chain integrity, network latency, or open-source test constraints. Within an Integrated Air and Missile Defense (IAMD) context, an interceptor round is not a standalone weapon; it is an endpoint effector within a multi-domain sensor-to-shooter network. Its operational reach is bounded by track-quality continuity, network data-link resilience, and command authorization rather than missile kinematics alone.

To evaluate SM-6 capabilities objectively, analysts must apply the 7-Rung Evidence Ladder, ranking public assertions strictly by the empirical weight of their supporting evidence.

       [RUNG 7] Sustained Combat Record (Real-world operational employment under contested conditions)
       [RUNG 6] DOT&E Operational Test (Formal operational effectiveness, suitability & survivability)
       [RUNG 5] Exercise Against Real Target (e.g., Tactical SINKEX outside scripted test ranges)
       [RUNG 4] Developmental / MDA Live Intercept (Scripted, controlled live-fire flight intercept)
       [RUNG 3] Simulated Engagement (Sensor, C2, synthetic track transfer & software validation)
       [RUNG 2] Concept Animation (Programmatic vision of multi-domain system integration)
       [RUNG 1] Vendor / Press Claim (Marketing positioning, unverified estimates, framework ceilings)

The 7-Rung Evidence Ladder

Rung Evidence Level Analytical Weight / What It Supports
1 Vendor or Press Claim Programmatic intent, unappropriated contract framework ceilings, or corporate positioning (e.g., "three missiles in one"). Cannot support operational capability claims.
2 Concept Animation Illustrates architectural vision for multi-layered integration (e.g., 2021 MDA Glide Phase Interceptor animation). Supports concept evaluation only.
3 Simulated Engagement Validates synthetic track transfer, command-and-control (C2) software pipelines, and fire-control logic (e.g., FTX-40, Valiant Shield 2024). Does not prove kinetic kill feasibility.
4 Developmental / MDA Live Intercept Demonstrates missile flight, target tracking, and active homing under tightly controlled, scripted test parameters (e.g., 2016 NIFC-CA, FTM-31 E1a, FTM-32).
5 Exercise Against Real Target Demonstrates tactical employment against real target hulls or drones outside developmental test scripts (e.g., Talisman Sabre 2025 SINKEX). Typically uses undefended, non-maneuvering targets.
6 DOT&E Operational Test Independent, rigorous evaluation establishing formal operational effectiveness, suitability, lethality, and survivability in contested environments (Incomplete for SM-6 BMD capabilities per FY2024 DOT&E reporting).
7 Sustained Combat Record Validates performance against unscripted, adaptive threat tactics under operational conditions (e.g., Red Sea engagements). Public metrics are aggregated or restricted.

Mandatory Silence Parameters

To maintain strict analytical rigor, defense briefers must enforce explicit silence boundaries. The following performance metrics are classified and must never be estimated, filled with unofficial open-source figures, or adopted from press speculation:

  1. Operational Range: Maximum kinematic, aerodynamic, or engagement distance (surface-launched, ground-launched, or air-launched).
  2. Engagement Envelope: Minimum/maximum engagement altitudes, slant ranges, radar cross-section boundaries, and cross-range limits.
  3. Seeker Performance & Logic: Mode-selection thresholds (active vs. semi-active blending), electronic counter-countermeasure (ECCM) processing, target discrimination algorithms, and seeker acquisition windows.
  4. Electronic Protection (EP) Features: Specific anti-jamming architectures, wave-form agility, cyber-resilience mechanisms, and signal-processing techniques in contested electromagnetic environments.
  5. Single-Shot Probability of Kill ($P_k$): Lethal warhead coupling, fuzing logic, and single-round engagement reliability.
  6. Precise Combat Expenditure Counts: Specific round counts expended during real-world engagements (e.g., Red Sea operational reporting aggregates SM-2 and SM-6 fires).
  7. Exact Block IB Post-Pause Schedule: Revised fleet delivery dates following the FY2026 budget strategic pause.

Detailed Event Narrative Analyses

1. 2016 NIFC-CA Intercept

Demonstrated Capability

Demonstrated the operational concept of Naval Integrated Fire Control-Counter Air (NIFC-CA) Engage-on-Remote (EoR). The Aegis Baseline 9 cruiser USS Princeton (CG-59) launched an SM-6 against an over-the-horizon, air-breathing target beyond the ship's organic radar horizon. Fire-control quality track custody was maintained by a remote airborne sensor (E-2D Advanced Hawkeye) and transmitted via the Cooperative Engagement Capability (CEC) data link directly into USS Princeton's combat system to command the engagement.

What Was NOT Established

This test did not establish engagement reliability or data-link continuity under contested electromagnetic conditions, active communications jamming, or degraded network throughput. It did not validate system performance against saturated, multi-directional air-warfare raids.

2. 2017 Tri-Mission Software Tests

Demonstrated Capability

Validated multi-mission software architecture within the baseline missile software family. Confirmed that a single missile baseline could ingest fire-control instructions across three distinct operational regimes: Anti-Air Warfare (AAW), Sea-Based Terminal Ballistic Missile Defense (SBT BMD), and Anti-Surface Warfare (SuW).

What Was NOT Established

This series did not establish universal operational effectiveness or single-shot kill probability across all operational threat profiles. Demonstrating multi-mission software logic in a controlled environment does not prove optimal target-coupling, fuzing performance, or kinetic lethality across every mission regime.

3. FTM-31 E1a (2023)

Demonstrated Capability

Demonstrated an endo-atmospheric terminal-phase intercept of a Medium-Range Ballistic Missile (MRBM) target using an SM-6 Dual II featuring Software Upgrade (SWUP) algorithms. The test successfully executed a two-interceptor salvo firing profile from an Aegis BMD-configured destroyer.

What Was NOT Established

This test did not establish the single-shot probability of kill ($P_k$) for an individual round, nor did it demonstrate magazine-efficient single-round intercepts. It did not establish fleet capability to defeat complex ballistic missile salvos, advanced countermeasure suites, decoys, or maneuvering re-entry vehicles.

4. FTM-32 (2024)

Demonstrated Capability

Demonstrated the detection, tracking, engagement, and live terminal-phase intercept of an advanced MRBM target off the coast of Kauai, Hawaii. The Aegis Baseline 9 destroyer USS Preble (DDG-88) launched a two-interceptor salvo of SM-6 Dual II SWUP rounds to execute the intercept. The event validated sensor-sharing and radar support from USS Jack H. Lucas (DDG-125)—the first Flight III Arleigh Burke destroyer equipped with the AN/SPY-6(V)1 radar—and integrated Allied interoperability contributions from Australian air, land, and sea assets, including the Anzac-class frigate HMAS Stewart, an E-7 Wedgetail airborne early warning aircraft, and an Australian ground-based radar.

What Was NOT Established

Per formal DOT&E evaluation, FTM-32 was "not designed to determine operational effectiveness, lethality, suitability, or survivability." The event was a developmental demonstration executed under controlled, scripted parameters and did not validate combat performance under operational stress, electronic attack, or complex raid scenarios.

5. Valiant Shield 2024

Demonstrated Capability

Demonstrated joint multi-domain architectural feasibility. Simulated tracking data generated by a Lower Tier Air and Missile Defense Sensor (LTAMDS) radar emulator were ingested by the U.S. Army's Integrated Battle Command System (IBCS) to issue launch and guidance commands using operational SM-6 engagement-control software in a synthetic flight environment.

What Was NOT Established

This event was a synthetic feasibility experiment. It did not establish a fielded U.S. Army SM-6 air-defense battery, live-fire missile launch from IBCS, or a completed operational evaluation of the joint Army-Navy kill chain.

6. FTX-40 (2025)

Demonstrated Capability

Demonstrated multi-domain tracking and fire-control integration against a maneuvering hypersonic-representative target. The test successfully established an end-to-end sensor-to-command pipeline, ingesting space-based track data from the Hypersonic and Ballistic Tracking Space Sensor (HBTSS) demonstration satellite, routing it through C2BMC and Build Option A (BOA) processing nodes, and handing off track custody to the Aegis Combat System to execute a simulated SM-6 engagement.

What Was NOT Established

FTX-40 did not launch a live missile or achieve a kinetic hypersonic intercept. It was explicitly a risk-reduction tracking and software integration event. It did not prove an operational, fielded counter-hypersonic weapon capability.

7. Talisman Sabre (2025)

Demonstrated Capability

Demonstrated land-based sea-denial employment. The U.S. Army Mid-Range Capability (Typhon / Mk 70-derived containerized launcher) fired a surface-launched SM-6 round that successfully struck and sank a decommissioned maritime vessel during an joint sinking exercise (SINKEX).

What Was NOT Established

This exercise did not establish anti-ship mission-kill effectiveness against a maneuvering, heavily defended peer surface combatant operating active electronic warfare, automated point-defense systems, and radar cross-section management.

Detailed Narrative Breakdown of Analytical Traps

  • Claim 1 Trap (Counter-Hypersonic Capability): Sits at Rung 3 (Simulated Engagement). Briefers frequently conflate synthetic tracking tests with physical kinetic kills. FTX-40 tracked a target and validated fire-control software, but no missile was launched. Operational counter-hypersonic interception remains unestablished on the public record.
  • Claim 2 Trap (Army Air Defense Configuration): Sits at Rung 3. Software linkage in Valiant Shield 2024 demonstrated architectural feasibility, not force structure. The deployed Army Typhon system utilizes Mk 70-derived containerized launchers for surface-to-surface strike, not a fielded air-defense battery integrated into IBCS and LTAMDS.
  • Claims 3 & 8 Traps (Classified Kinematic Parameters): Violates explicit silence protocol. Unverified open-source media figures (e.g., "460 km" or "300 nm") are speculative estimates. Official U.S. Navy Fact Files intentionally omit range figures for SM-6 and AIM-174B.
  • Claim 4 Trap (Contract Framework Ceilings vs. Appropriations): Sits at Rung 1 (Vendor Claim). RTX's $24.4B multi-year framework agreement establishes industrial output capacity exceeding 500 rounds per year, but actual missile deliveries are strictly bounded by annual enacted congressional appropriations (e.g., 540 rounds requested in FY2027) and a 36-to-39-month production lead time.
  • Claims 5 & 10 Traps (DOT&E Operational Evaluation vs. Developmental Flight): Conflating developmental success (Rung 4) with formal operational evaluation (Rung 6). FTM-32 was a developmental test; DOT&E explicitly confirmed it was not designed to evaluate operational effectiveness or survivability. Furthermore, DOT&E FY2022 and FY2024 reports explicitly noted insufficient data to assess system survivability in contested electromagnetic or cyber environments.
  • Claim 6 Trap (Outdated Programmatic Projections): Relying on historical schedule projections. While FY2022 DOT&E documentation cited an FY2027 fleet introduction for Block IB, subsequent FY2026 budget documentation instituted a "strategic pause" and reduced development scope, rendering legacy timelines invalid.
  • Claim 7 Trap (Uncontested SINKEX Extrapolation): Sits at Rung 5 (Exercise Against Real Target). Striking a static, undefended target hull proves basic guidance and kinetic impact, but cannot support claims of mission-kill effectiveness against a active, defended warship.
  • Claim 9 Trap (Economic Cost-Basis Confusion): Quoting constant-dollar lifecycle program averages ($2.97M constant FY2004 APUC across a baseline 2,478-round procurement) as contemporary procurement costs, ignoring inflation and fiscal year variances that place then-year flyaway costs near $4.3M in FY2025/FY2026 budget line items.

4. The Fluency Falsification Test: Specifications vs. Operational Evidence

The Core Falsification Rule

"If an analyst can recite physical specifications (e.g., 21 ft 6 in length, 3,300 lb weight, dual-thrust motor) but cannot state what FTM-32 or FTX-40 did NOT establish, the analyst has failed operational fluency."

Critical Architectural Distinctions

1. Fire-Control Concepts: Launch-on-Remote (LoR) vs. Engage-on-Remote (EoR)

True operational fluency requires distinguishing between two distinct network-enabled fire-control concepts: * Launch-on-Remote (LoR): The launching platform fires the interceptor based on offboard track data provided by a remote sensor, but must subsequently establish organic radar track custody of the target to provide mid-course updates or illuminate the target for terminal intercept. * Engage-on-Remote (EoR): The launching platform fires and guides the interceptor entirely using fire-control quality data supplied by offboard sensors (e.g., E-2D via CEC or HBTSS via C2BMC/BOA) without the launching ship ever establishing organic radar tracking custody. The missile relies entirely on offboard network data until its active radar seeker acquires the target in the terminal phase.

Analytical Takeaway: Network latency, data-link disruption, or loss of offboard track continuity immediately collapses an EoR engagement, shrinking the interceptor's operational reach back to the organic radar horizon regardless of missile motor kinematics.

2. Upgrade Axes: Software Iteration vs. Kinematic Propulsion Redesign

Analysts must differentiate between software/electronics evolution and physical propulsion redesigns: * Software/Electronics Upgrades (e.g., Dual II SWUP, Block IAU): Iterate on established hardware baselines by modifying mission logic, active seeker processing, and guidance algorithms. These carry relatively low integration risk and move at software deployment speeds. * Kinematic Propulsion Redesigns (e.g., Block IB): Replace the standard 13.5-inch rocket motor with a 21-inch second-stage rocket motor. Altering missile diameter and propulsion energy introduces structural, thermal, energetic, insensitive-munitions, and flight-qualification risks. These physical engineering challenges inherently trigger schedule instability and programmatic delays (e.g., the FY2026 strategic pause).

Correcting "Spec-Dumping" in Executive Briefings

When auditing defense briefings that over-index on memorized physical parameters at the expense of network boundaries, enforce three analytical corrections:

  1. Reframe Reach around Network Integrity: When a brief cites "extended operational reach," force the briefer to identify the offboard sensor, network protocol (e.g., CEC), and maximum tolerable data-link latency required to support Launch-on-Remote or Engage-on-Remote flight.
  2. Standardize Cost Metrics: Reject isolated unit cost figures. Require briefers to explicitly state the accounting basis (Program APUC vs. Flyaway Cost) and fiscal year dollars (Constant FY2004 vs. Contemporary Then-Year).
  3. Audit Flight Claims Against DOT&E Reports: Cross-reference every claims of "demonstrated capability" against annual DOT&E reports to verify whether an event was a controlled developmental flight (Rung 4) or a formal operational test (Rung 6).

Signals to Watch (Known-By Checklist)

To evaluate changes in SM-6 operational and programmatic status over time, monitor these explicit milestones:

  • FTM-43 Flight Test Results: Tracks whether counter-hypersonic intercept performance transitions from Rung 3 (Simulated Engagement) to Rung 4 (Developmental Live Intercept).
  • Official Block IB Restart Decision: Clarifies whether the 21-inch motor kinematic redesign resumes active flight qualification or remains paused following the FY2026 budget strategic pause.
  • Annual DOT&E Reports: Serves as the authoritative source for Rung 6 assessments regarding formal operational effectiveness, suitability, lethality, and survivability in contested environments.
  • Enacted FY2027 Defense Appropriations: Determines whether requested procurement quantities (e.g., 540 rounds) convert into fully funded production orders line items, factoring in the 36-to-39-month manufacturing lead time.
