Day blocks

Block 3: Active practice

Time: 90 minutes Goal: Deliberate practice with feedback Pack: Raytheon SM-6 (sm-6)

Focus from mind map

Four drills. Do each closed-book first, then check against the spine page and score yourself.

Sources

  • SM-6 assessment page, sections "Command layer", "Variant roadmap", "Evidence and test record", "Comparative position"
  • MDA, FTM-32 (video)
  • MDA, Multi-Mission Warfare events with SM-6 Dual I (video)
  • CSIS Missile Threat, SM-6

Actions

Drill A: kill chain from memory (20m)

On a blank page, write the seven-step engagement sequence. For each step, name the system that does it in NIFC-CA and one dependency that can fail. Score one point per step, system, and dependency (21 points).

Drill B: grade ten claims (25m)

For each claim write a status (demonstrated, demonstrated with limits, not public, or false as stated), the event that supports it, and what the event did not establish.

  1. SM-6 has intercepted a ballistic missile in its terminal phase.
  2. SM-6 is proven against hypersonic glide vehicles.
  3. SM-6 can hit a target the firing ship's radar cannot see.
  4. SM-6's range is 460 km.
  5. SM-6 can sink a ship.
  6. The Army fields an SM-6 air-defense battery tied into IBCS.
  7. Block IB reaches the fleet in FY2027.
  8. SM-6 has passed operational testing in its BMD role.
  9. Raytheon will build more than 500 SM-6 a year under a $24.4 billion deal.
  10. The AIM-174B is deployed.

Drill C: variant family tree (20m)

Draw two axes. X runs from "electronics or software change" to "kinematic change". Y lists missions added. Place Block I, Block IA, Dual I, Dual II and SWUP, Block IAU, Block IB, and AIM-174B. Check against "Variant roadmap".

Drill D: layer table (25m)

For six threat classes, name the layer or effector family the open sources point to, and give the reason. Mark any row where the sources are silent. Stay at the level of the spine page's "Comparative position" and "Saturation and economics" sections.

Retrieval check

Question

What was the hardest practice step, and what mistake did you correct?

Answer cues

Drill A: detect and classify (SPY radar, E-2D, space sensors), share track (CEC), threat evaluation and weapon assignment (Aegis Baseline 9), launch (Mk 41), in-flight updates (datalink), terminal homing (SM-6 active or semi-active seeker), kill assessment (sensors). Dependencies include track quality, network latency, identity confidence, cell availability, and seeker acquisition. Drill B: 1 demonstrated (FTM-31 E1a 2023, FTM-32 2024), not operational effectiveness. 2 not public (FTX-40 simulated). 3 demonstrated (2016 NIFC-CA), not under every contested condition. 4 not public (range is classified). 5 demonstrated with limits (Talisman Sabre 2025 sinking), not against a defended peer warship. 6 false as stated (Valiant Shield 2024 was a simulated feasibility demo). 7 superseded (2025 strategic pause). 8 not established (DOT&E FY2024). 9 demonstrated with limits (framework ceiling and objective, not funded quantity; FY2027 request is 540). 10 demonstrated (Navy acknowledges deployment, range classified). Drill D: close-in cruise missile goes to ESSM or SM-2 to save SM-6. Over-the-horizon cruise missile is the NIFC-CA case for SM-6. Terminal SRBM is SM-6 Sea-Based Terminal. Midcourse MRBM is SM-3. Surface combatant at range is SM-6 or a purpose-built anti-ship weapon, with damage effect scenario-dependent. Cheap drones point to lower-cost layers (guns, EW, short-range interceptors). The sources give principles, not firing doctrine. Common miss: grading claims 5 or 9 as fully demonstrated.

Exit criteria

  • Drill A scored (target 18 of 21)
  • Drill B: ten claims graded with an event named for each
  • Variant tree drawn
  • Layer table filled, silences marked
