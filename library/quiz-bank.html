Quiz bank — Raytheon SM-6

Short retrieval items for blocks 3, 4, and 6. Answer closed-book, then open the key.

Items

  1. What two older missiles supplied SM-6's airframe lineage and its seeker?
  2. When did SM-6 reach initial operational capability?
  3. Name the three core missions, and the two newer extensions.
  4. Why does an active seeker raise the number of engagements a ship can support?
  5. SM-6 versus SM-3: which kills inside the atmosphere, and by what mechanism does each kill?
  6. What does NIFC-CA let a ship do that its own radar cannot?
  7. Which system fuses sensor data from many platforms into one track?
  8. What did FTM-32 (March 2024) demonstrate?
  9. What did DOT&E say FTM-32 was not designed to determine?
  10. What did FTX-40 (March 2025) demonstrate, and what did it not?
  11. Which Army system fires SM-6 from a container, and what else does it fire?
  12. What happened to Block IB in the FY2026 budget?
  13. Why are $2.97M and $4.3M both cited as SM-6 unit cost?
  14. What is the scarce resource that limits SM-6 use at sea, apart from money?
  15. Name the seven steps of the engagement sequence.
Answer key
  1. SM-2 Block IV airframe; AMRAAM-derived active seeker.
  2. November 2013.
  3. Extended-range air warfare, terminal BMD, anti-surface warfare. Extensions: counter-hypersonic (in development) and AIM-174B air launch.
  4. It does not need continuous ship illumination at the end, so finite illuminators are not the bottleneck.
  5. SM-6 inside (blast-fragmentation). SM-3 outside (hit-to-kill).
  6. Engage a target beyond its radar horizon using a remote sensor's track (for example an E-2D).
  7. CEC.
  8. Detect, track, engage, and live-intercept an advanced MRBM target in terminal phase with SM-6 Dual II SWUP from an Aegis Baseline 9 ship.
  9. Operational effectiveness, lethality, suitability, or survivability.
  10. Detection, tracking, space-sensor data in fire control, and a simulated engagement of a maneuvering hypersonic-representative target. No live intercept.
  11. Typhon (Mid-Range Capability). Tomahawk.
  12. Funding cut and a "strategic pause". Motor work continued; no public restart as of Oct 2026.
  13. Different bases: average program unit cost in constant FY2004 dollars versus then-year budget cost per round.
  14. Mk 41 cells. One round per cell, and reloading at sea is constrained.
  15. Detect and classify, share track, evaluate and assign, launch, update in flight, terminal homing, kill assessment.
