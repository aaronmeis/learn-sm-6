Block 1: Core concepts

Time: 60 minutes Goal: Lock vocabulary and foundations Pack: Raytheon SM-6 (sm-6)

Focus from mind map

  • Identity: RIM-174 Extended Range Active Missile. SM-2 Block IV airframe lineage plus an AMRAAM-derived active radar seeker. Development began 2004. IOC November 2013.
  • Missions: extended-range air warfare, terminal ballistic-missile defense (Sea-Based Terminal), and anti-surface warfare. Counter-hypersonic work is in development. AIM-174B carries the seeker into air launch.
  • Kill-chain parts: SPY-1 or SPY-6 radar, E-2D Advanced Hawkeye, Cooperative Engagement Capability (CEC), Aegis Baseline 9, NIFC-CA, Mk 41 VLS, dual-mode seeker, blast-fragmentation warhead.
  • Joint and land: Typhon (Army Mid-Range Capability), IBCS, LTAMDS.

Sources

  • SM-6 assessment page, sections "Executive assessment", "Identity and mission", "System architecture"
  • U.S. Navy Fact File, Standard Missile
  • CSIS Missile Threat, Standard Missile-6
  • CRS RL33745, Navy Aegis BMD Program
  • CSIS, The Aegis Approach with RADM Tom Druggan (video)

Actions

  1. 15m. Read "Executive assessment" and "Identity and mission". Write one line each for: RIM-174 ERAM, active homing, semi-active homing, blast-fragmentation, hit-to-kill, endo-atmospheric, exo-atmospheric, terminal phase.
  2. 20m. Read "System architecture" (missile, sensor, command, launch layers). Write one line each for: Aegis Baseline 9, SPY-6, E-2D, CEC, NIFC-CA, engage-on-remote, launch-on-remote, Mk 41 VLS, Typhon, IBCS, LTAMDS, AIM-174B.
  3. 15m. Watch the first 20 minutes of the CSIS Aegis talk. Add any term the speaker uses that is not on your list.
  4. 10m. Mark three terms you cannot yet explain without the page. Expected weak spots: engage-on-remote versus launch-on-remote, CEC versus NIFC-CA, and what "Dual II SWUP" means.

Retrieval check

Question

Without notes, name five core concepts in Raytheon SM-6 and define one in a sentence.

Answer cues

Active seeker: the missile's own radar finds the target at the end, so the ship's illuminators are freed and the shot can finish past the ship's radar horizon. Semi-active: the missile homes on energy the ship bounces off the target. Blast-fragmentation: a proximity warhead that works inside the atmosphere against aircraft, cruise missiles, terminal ballistic targets, and ships. SM-3 uses hit-to-kill outside the atmosphere instead. CEC: fuses sensor data from many ships and aircraft into one fire-control-quality track. NIFC-CA: the engage-on-remote kill chain built from E-2D, CEC, Aegis Baseline 9, and SM-6. Mk 41: the vertical launch cell. One SM-6 uses one cell. Typhon: the Army's containerized launcher that fires SM-6 and Tomahawk.

Exit criteria

  • Concept list written (at least 15 terms)
  • Three weak terms marked

Block 2: Mental models

Time: 45 minutes Goal: How experts chunk the domain Pack: Raytheon SM-6 (sm-6)

Focus from mind map

Five models carry the whole day. Each one answers a different question.

# Model Question it answers Failure mode
M1 Kill web, not missile. Capability is the weakest link of Sense, Share, Decide, Act, Assess. How far can SM-6 reach, and what breaks it? Crediting the missile for reach the network provides. Forgetting that network loss shrinks the reach.
M2 Layers by phase and altitude. SM-3 exo-atmospheric midcourse, future GPI in glide phase, SM-6 endo-atmospheric terminal, ESSM and SM-2 inner layers. Where does SM-6 sit, and what does it not replace? Treating SM-6 as a substitute for broad-area midcourse defense.
M3 Evidence ladder. Vendor claim, concept animation, simulated engagement, developmental live intercept, DOT&E operational test, combat record. How much weight can this claim carry? Reading FTX-40 as a live intercept. Reading "combat-proven" as an operational-test pass.
M4 Two upgrade axes. Electronics and software (Block IA, Dual I and II, SWUP, IAU) versus kinematics (Block IB's new motor). How risky is this roadmap item? Expecting a new-motor program to move at software speed.
M5 Magazine economics. The VLS cell is the scarce unit. Cost-exchange ratios ignore the value of what is protected. When is SM-6 worth firing? "Too expensive to use" or "always fire the best round." Both skip the layer question.

Sources

  • SM-6 assessment page, sections "Performance logic", "Saturation and economics", "Variant roadmap", "Enterprise-architecture view"
  • MDA, Glide Phase Interceptor scenario animation (video)
  • CSIS, Cost and Value in Air and Missile Defense Intercepts
  • DOT&E FY2024 SM-6
  • elicit-light-research (Adams et al. 2025: each kill-chain link multiplies the odds)

Actions

  1. 10m. Watch the MDA GPI animation (8 minutes). Sketch the layers it shows and where SM-6 fires.
  2. 20m. For each model M1 to M5, write When to use, How to apply, and one SM-6 example from the spine page.
  3. 15m. Draw how the models connect. M1 explains reach and fragility. M2 places SM-6 in the stack. M3 gates every claim. M4 explains roadmap risk. M5 limits use. Map M1 onto the six-row Sense, Share, Decide, Act, Assess, Sustain table on the spine page.

Retrieval check

Question

Someone says "SM-6 can kill hypersonic missiles." Which mental model do you reach for first, and why?

Answer cues

M3 first. FTX-40 (March 2025) tracked a maneuvering hypersonic-representative target and ran a simulated SM-6 engagement. No missile was fired. MDA called it a building block toward a live intercept, and no live intercept was public as of October 2026. Then M2. SM-6's counter-hypersonic role is a limited terminal layer. The Glide Phase Interceptor is meant to engage earlier. Then M1. The chain used space-sensor data (HBTSS), so the claim also depends on sensors the missile does not own.

Exit criteria

  • M1 to M5 captured with when, how, and failure
  • One diagram connecting the models

  2. SM-6 is proven against hypersonic glide vehicles.

Retrieval check

Question

What was the hardest practice step, and what mistake did you correct?

Answer cues

Drill A: detect and classify (SPY radar, E-2D, space sensors), share track (CEC), threat evaluation and weapon assignment (Aegis Baseline 9), launch (Mk 41), in-flight updates (datalink), terminal homing (SM-6 active or semi-active seeker), kill assessment (sensors). Dependencies include track quality, network latency, identity confidence, cell availability, and seeker acquisition. Drill B: 1 demonstrated (FTM-31 E1a 2023, FTM-32 2024), not operational effectiveness. 2 not public (FTX-40 simulated). 3 demonstrated (2016 NIFC-CA), not under every contested condition. 4 not public (range is classified). 5 demonstrated with limits (Talisman Sabre 2025 sinking), not against a defended peer warship. 6 false as stated (Valiant Shield 2024 was a simulated feasibility demo). 7 superseded (2025 strategic pause). 8 not established (DOT&E FY2024). 9 demonstrated with limits (framework ceiling and objective, not funded quantity; FY2027 request is 540). 10 demonstrated (Navy acknowledges deployment, range classified). Drill D: close-in cruise missile goes to ESSM or SM-2 to save SM-6. Over-the-horizon cruise missile is the NIFC-CA case for SM-6. Terminal SRBM is SM-6 Sea-Based Terminal. Midcourse MRBM is SM-3. Surface combatant at range is SM-6 or a purpose-built anti-ship weapon, with damage effect scenario-dependent. Cheap drones point to lower-cost layers (guns, EW, short-range interceptors). The sources give principles, not firing doctrine. Common miss: grading claims 5 or 9 as fully demonstrated.

Exit criteria

  • Drill A scored (target 18 of 21)
  • Drill B: ten claims graded with an event named for each
  • Variant tree drawn
  • Layer table filled, silences marked

Block 4: Teach it

Time: 30 minutes Goal: Explain aloud or write a teach-back Pack: Raytheon SM-6 (sm-6)

Focus from mind map

  • One sentence to anchor: SM-6 is a high-value, multi-domain effector whose performance scales with the kill web around it.
  • Teach it as an endpoint in a network, not as a missile spec sheet.

Sources

  • SM-6 assessment page, sections "Executive assessment" and "Bottom line"
  • Your block 2 model table and block 3 drill scores

Actions

  1. 10m. Teach the five-minute version aloud with no notes. Record it if you can. - Hook (30s): "It's sold as three missiles in one. A better description is one endpoint in a kill web." - What it is (60s): SM-2 airframe lineage, AMRAAM-derived active seeker, blast-fragmentation warhead, Mk 41 cell, plus Typhon and AIM-174B. - How it reaches (90s): NIFC-CA and sensor-shooter separation. Three gains: earlier engagement, a larger defended footprint, and more engagements because the seeker frees the ship's illuminators. - Proven versus not (90s): FTM-32 live terminal MRBM intercept, FTX-40 simulated only, DOT&E says operational effectiveness is not yet established. - Limits (60s): one round per cell, cost, offboard-track dependence, contested-spectrum unknowns, Block IB paused.
  2. 10m. Give the two-minute executive version. Cut the systems names and keep the claims.
  3. 10m. List the points where you stalled. Fix each with a short source check, not a full re-read.

Retrieval check

Question

Explain Raytheon SM-6 to a sharp beginner in under two minutes. Where did you stall?

Answer cues

"SM-6 is a Navy missile that now also flies from Army launchers and fighter jets. It does three jobs: shoots down aircraft and cruise missiles at long range, stops some ballistic missiles in the last seconds of flight, and hits ships. What makes it special is its own radar seeker, which lets a ship fire using someone else's sensor, like an E-2D, and hit a target its own radar can't see. That reach depends on the network, so jamming or losing the network shrinks it. Tests prove a lot, but the independent tester says full operational effectiveness isn't established, and a live hypersonic intercept hasn't been shown publicly. Each round costs millions and takes a launch cell, so the Navy saves it for the hard targets." Typical stalls: explaining engage-on-remote without jargon, and saying why the seeker frees illuminators.

Exit criteria

  • Five-minute teach-back done
  • Two-minute version done
  • Gaps listed and fixed

Block 5: Edge cases

Time: 45 minutes Goal: Exceptions, traps, failure modes Pack: Raytheon SM-6 (sm-6)

Focus from mind map

Most SM-6 mistakes are about evidence and numbers, not engineering. Work the trap table below.

Sources

  • SM-6 assessment page, sections "Risks and unknowns", "Evidence and test record", "Deployment and industrial base"
  • DOT&E FY2024 SM-6
  • DOT&E FY2022 SM-6
  • MDA FTX-40 release
  • Naval News, Block IB strategic pause
  • CSIS, Rebuilding U.S. Missile Inventory (May 2026)

Actions

  1. 15m. Read the two DOT&E reports (two pages each) and "Risks and unknowns".
  2. 20m. Cover the right two columns of the table. For each symptom, say the cause and the fix aloud, then check.
  3. 10m. Add your top three traps to cheatsheet.
# Symptom Cause Fix
1 A source states a precise SM-6 range Range is classified. Explainer videos repeat unofficial estimates. Say "classified; open-source estimates vary" and cite no number as fact.
2 "SM-6 is proven against hypersonics" FTX-40 was tracking plus a simulated engagement. FTM-43 not publicly flown. Place the claim on the evidence ladder. Say "limited terminal defense, live intercept pending".
3 "Combat-proven" used as "operationally effective" Combat use and DOT&E operational test answer different questions. Cite DOT&E FY2024: FTM-32 was not designed to determine effectiveness, lethality, suitability, or survivability.
4 Block IB "arrives FY2027" DOT&E FY2022 date, superseded by the FY2026 budget pause. Treat Block IB as developmental. Watch for a restart decision.
5 Unit cost quoted as $2.97M, $4.3M, or higher Constant FY2004 APUC, then-year budget cost, and other bases differ. State the basis and year with every number.
6 "$24.4B for 500 a year" read as funded buys Framework ceiling and production objective are not appropriations. Pair it with the FY2027 request (540) and the 36 to 39 month lead time.
7 Talisman Sabre sinking read as "kills warships" A sinking exercise target is not a defended, maneuvering peer combatant. Say "demonstrated strike, effect against defended ships not public".
8 "One missile replaces SM-2, SM-3, ESSM" Multi-mission does not mean optimal for every layer. One round uses one cell. Use the layer model. SM-3 owns midcourse. ESSM gives magazine density.
9 Intact missile assumed to mean intact engagement Over-the-horizon shots depend on remote track, network, identity, authorization. Ask which link of Sense, Share, Decide, Act is assumed.
10 Contested-spectrum or cyber resilience asserted either way DOT&E found insufficient public data. Classified data may exist. Say "uncertain on the public record", not "fails" or "immune".
11 Variant names mixed up Air-warfare blocks, BMD configurations, and software upgrades overlap. Read the variant roadmap as a family tree with two upgrade axes.
12 SM-6-only combat expenditure quoted Public Red Sea and Epic Fury figures combine SM-2 and SM-6 or are estimates. Name the source and say "combined" or "estimated".

Retrieval check

Question

What edge case would break a naive approach to Raytheon SM-6?

Answer cues

Treating a demonstration as operational effectiveness (traps 2, 3, 7). Quoting a classified number as fact (trap 1). Mixing cost bases (trap 5). Each one makes a brief look confident and be wrong.

Exit criteria

  • Twelve traps worked closed-book
  • Top three traps on the cheatsheet
